\subsection{Population-Level Analysis Confirms Learning in Delay Conditioning and Short-Trace Conditioning}
% region Population-Level Analysis
% Transition from representative single-fish examples to population-level analysis
The examples above illustrate the form of the behavioral change in individual larvae, but they do not establish whether it is reproducible across animals. We therefore quantified CS-aligned tail movement across larvae within each conditioning condition and compared paired groups with their matched unpaired controls. This population-level analysis tested whether CS--US pairing produced a consistent change in tail-movement vigor and whether that change persisted when the CS was subsequently presented alone.

% Define trace intervals and the comparison of short- and long-trace conditioning
In the trace-conditioning protocols, the CS and US did not overlap: the US was delivered either \SI{3}{\second} (3sTrace) or \SI{10}{\second} (10sTrace) after CS offset. Comparing these conditions with Delay conditioning allowed us to ask whether the assay supported learning across a short stimulus-free interval and whether performance changed when that interval was extended.

% region Population Heatmaps
% Introduce CS-aligned population heatmaps
\paragraph{Population Heatmaps}

% Explain active-fish interpretation of scaled-vigor heatmaps
For each condition, we aligned scaled-vigor measurements to CS onset and aggregated the data across larvae (Materials and Methods). The resulting heatmaps show the mean scaled vigor among fish that moved within each time bin. Because vigor is undefined during immobility, these plots describe movement intensity in the active subset of fish rather than the fraction of the population that moved. Consequently, a non-dark bin does not imply that all larvae were active; it may reflect residual movement in a small subset of fish.

% Compare Delay and 3sTrace heatmaps
\paragraph{Delay and 3sTrace Conditioning}

% Describe heatmap evidence for acquisition and extinction in paired groups
In the Delay and 3sTrace groups (\FIG{fig2} A,B), the CS--US pairing was followed by a progressive reduction in scaled vigor during training. The reduction was most evident around the part of the trial preceding the expected US, persisted into the first CS-only test trials, and weakened over later test trials. Thus, the heatmaps provided a qualitative population-level signature of acquisition followed by extinction in both the delay and short-trace protocols.

% Contrast paired groups with matched unpaired controls
The corresponding unpaired controls did not show a sustained CS-locked reduction in vigor (\FIG{fig2} A--C). Their CS-aligned activity was broadly stable across the experiment, apart from a brief increase during CS presentation. Because US presentations were intentionally unpaired and occurred at different times in different control larvae, US-evoked responses were temporally dispersed in CS-aligned population heatmaps. The contrast with the paired groups therefore supports the interpretation that the suppression was linked to the CS--US contingency rather than to repeated stimulation or a general change in motor output.

% Describe 10sTrace as the third main Figure 2 comparison
\paragraph{10sTrace Conditioning}

% Report the weak and inconclusive 10sTrace population pattern
The 10sTrace condition was retained as the third comparison in Figure 2 (\FIG{fig2} C) to test the boundary of the assay across a longer stimulus-free interval. Extending the trace interval to \SI{10}{\second} markedly reduced the population-level effect. The 10sTrace heatmaps showed, at most, a weak reduction in vigor during the interval between CS offset and US onset. This pattern was not consistently distinguishable from the matched control response at the population level. Thus, although 10sTrace is presented alongside Delay and 3sTrace, it does not provide clear population-level evidence of conditioning under the present protocol; the data nevertheless do not exclude learning in individual larvae or under different training conditions.

% Describe UR alignment and the US-aligned control analysis
\paragraph{Unconditioned Responses and Control Alignment}

% Explain why the UR is dispersed in CS-aligned control heatmaps
In paired groups, the optovin-mediated unconditioned response (UR) remained visible during training as a band of elevated vigor at the expected US time. In contrast, the temporally varied US schedule in the unpaired controls dispersed the UR in CS-aligned plots. When control data were aligned to US onset, activity before the US showed no systematic suppression or build-up (Sup. Fig. ???). This analysis provides an additional control for the possibility that changes in the paired groups were driven by events other than the CS--US contingency.

% region Block-Level Statistics
% Introduce predefined block-level quantification of learned suppression and extinction
\paragraph{Block-Level Quantification of Conditioned Suppression}

% Define the response-over-baseline CR ratio and Pre-Train, Early Test, and Late Test blocks
To quantify the population effect, we calculated a CR ratio by dividing vigor in the prespecified response window by vigor in the pre-CS baseline window (Materials and Methods). We compared three predefined five-trial blocks: the final five pre-training trials (Pre-Train, PT), the first five test trials (Early Test, ET), and the final five test trials (Late Test, LT). The ET block assessed expression of a learned response in the absence of the US, whereas the LT block assessed its decline during repeated CS-alone presentations.

% Quantify Delay-group CR expression and extinction
In the Delay group, the CR ratio was lower during ET than during PT and than in the matched unpaired control group (\FIG{fig2} D). This difference shows that the CS acquired the capacity to suppress tail-movement vigor after paired training. By LT, the Delay-group ratio had returned toward its PT value, consistent with extinction during testing.
% todo add the statistical result for the Delay block comparison

% Quantify 3sTrace CR expression and extinction
The 3sTrace group showed the same qualitative pattern in the block-level analysis: the CR ratio was reduced during ET relative to PT and to the matched unpaired control group (\FIG{fig2} E). The reduction was no longer evident in LT, indicating that the learned suppression declined as testing proceeded without the US. Together with the heatmaps, these comparisons show that larvae can form an association when a \SI{3}{\second} stimulus-free interval separates the CS and US.
% todo add the statistical result for the 3sTrace block comparison

% Report the absence of a reliable 10sTrace population effect
In contrast, the 10sTrace group did not show a statistically reliable ET reduction in CR ratio relative to its PT baseline and matched control under the present analysis (\FIG{fig2} F). The extended trace interval therefore represents a condition in which the current experiment did not detect a consistent population-level conditioned response, rather than a demonstrated upper limit on the larvae's capacity for temporal association.

% region Trial-Resolved Analysis
% Introduce trial-resolved acquisition and extinction analysis
\paragraph{Acquisition and Extinction Across Trials}

% Describe the trial-resolved course of acquisition and extinction
Trial-resolved CR ratios complemented the block comparisons by showing how the group response developed across the experiment (\FIG{fig2} G--I). In the Delay and 3sTrace groups, the CS-locked reduction emerged during training, was expressed during the first CS-only test trials, and then declined over subsequent test trials. The unpaired controls remained close to their pre-training values across the same phases. These time courses are consistent with gradual acquisition of conditioned suppression and its extinction when the CS was no longer followed by the US.

% Summarize the population-level result and separate it from later individual and timing analyses
The population analyses establish the central behavioral result of this study: Delay conditioning and 3sTrace conditioning produce a CS-locked reduction in tail-movement vigor that is absent in matched unpaired controls and diminishes during testing. The magnitude and timing of this response varied across individual larvae; these features are considered separately below rather than inferred from the population average alone.

% region Control Specificity
% Test whether violet light alone can act as the US
\paragraph{Control Specificity: Violet Light Without Optovin}

% Report the violet-light-only control
In a preliminary control experiment, larvae received Delay-style CS--US pairings with \SI{100}{\milli\second} violet-light pulses in the absence of optovin. Violet light alone did not produce a detectable conditioned response (Sup. Fig. 3). This result supports the conclusion that reinforcement in the assay depends on optovin-mediated photostimulation rather than on the violet light pulse alone.
