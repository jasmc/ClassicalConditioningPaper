\paragraph{Setup, CS, and US}
% Describe head-fixed preparation and tail tracking
In the experimental apparatus, a zebrafish larva was head-restrained in an agarose gel, with its eyes and tail free to move. Tail kinematics were recorded by automatically tracking the tail.
% review: revise "head-restrained in an agarose gel" to "embedded in agarose with the eyes and tail free to move" for conventional preparation terminology.
% todo cite Fig. 1A and add a head-fixed larval-zebrafish behavioral-tracking reference if this preparation is not fully introduced in Materials and Methods
% Define CS illumination and duration
White or red LEDs were used as visual stimuli. A pair of LEDs in front of the larvae provided constant basal illumination; this illumination switched off when the pair of LEDs on the right side turned on (represented in green in \FIG{fig1}A) for \SI{10}{\second}, which served as the conditioned stimulus (CS; Materials and Methods).
% Define optovin-based US and behavioral response
Fish medium contained \SI{10}{\micro\Molar} optovin. For conditioning, \SI{100}{\milli\second} violet-light pulses triggered the optovin-mediated stimulation (\FIG{fig1}A; Materials and Methods). This consistently produced vigorous tail responses (Sup. Fig.~1).

% region Experimental Protocols
\paragraph{Experimental Protocols}
% Outline the four main phases of the conditioning protocol
Overall, conditioning experiments included four main phases (\FIG{fig1} B; Sup. Fig. 2 A; Materials and Methods):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Priming/habituation, 4 CS-like stimuli alone to habituate larvae to the CS;
  \item Pre-training, 10 CS alone;
  \item Training, 46 CS-US paired trials (using \SI{100}{\milli\second} long violet light pulses for the US, \textit{long US}) and 4 CS alone (catch trials); and
  \item Testing, 30 CS alone.
\end{enumerate}

Optovin stimuli not only evoked robust and vigorous tail responses (Sup. Fig.~1), but also elevated baseline swimming activity to levels unusual for head-fixed larvae (Sup. Fig.~2 B).

% Explain rationale for adding short USs to prime and sustain activity
This was an important consideration for our behavioral readout, which was based on tail movement vigor, and greatly influenced protocol design (Sup. Fig. 2 A; Materials and Methods). We therefore strategically incorporated \SI{50}{\milli\second} long violet light pulses (\textit{short US}) at key points in the protocol to first prime the larvae into an active behavioral state of high baseline movement rate (during the priming/habituation phase) and then to maintain it throughout the experiment. These short USs were never used for conditioning; they were never paired with the CS across trials and were temporally separated from any CS presentation by >30~s (Materials and Methods).

% Describe randomized unpaired Control protocol
For the control, we designed a CS-US unpaired condition in which CS presentation timings were randomized during the training phase (\FIG{fig1} B; Materials and Methods) to preclude associative learning. To further minimize the possibility of accidental conditioning, we assigned nearly every Control fish a unique, randomized CS presentation schedule (Materials and Methods).


\subsection{Suppression of Tail Movement Vigor as the Conditioned Response}
% Define learning metric and tail vigor measure
We assessed learning, in this case the capacity to form associations between the visual CS and the optovin-based US, by measuring changes in tail movement vigor (rate of change of overall tail curvature over time; Materials and Methods) evoked by the CS over the course of conditioning.

% region CRs in Single Fish
\paragraph{Example of a Potential Learner: Qualitative Analysis of the CR}
% Motivate qualitative single-fish inspection for CR emergence
To gain initial insights into learning-related behavioral changes (appearance of conditioned responses, CRs), we first examined data from individual fish qualitatively.
% Describe baseline normalization of trial-wise vigor
Tail movement vigor of individual trials was normalized to the corresponding baseline vigor preceding CS onset (\FIG{fig1} D; Materials and Methods). This normalization accounted for natural oscillations in baseline tail movement across fish and trials (\FIG{fig1} D,E; Materials and Methods).
% review: Figure 1D should show the vigor calculation; cite Fig. 1E and Fig. 1F only when discussing the respective individual heatmaps.
% Explain heatmap visualization for qualitative assessment
Visualizing baseline-normalized vigor as trial-by-time heatmaps allowed an initial qualitative assessment of CS-evoked responses across the experiment (\FIG{fig1} E--G).
% review: define the exact baseline-normalization formula once in Materials and Methods and use the same terminology in the Figure 1 legend.
% Primary 3sTrace fish showing learned suppression during conditioning and testing
We found a distinct behavioral pattern in an example 3sTrace fish (\FIG{fig1} F; final panel assignment to confirm). During pre-training, CS presentation did not elicit a detectable modulation of tail vigor. With training, vigor after CS onset progressively decreased across trials. In the initial test trials, this reduction extended beyond CS offset into the stimulus-free trace interval, consistent with acquisition and short-term expression of a conditioned suppression response.
% todo confirm the final Fig. 1F 3sTrace heatmap and quantify the response within the trace interval if this temporal claim is retained
% review: retain this as a qualitative illustration of temporal bridging; the matched population analyses in Results 2, not one example fish, should support the associative-learning claim.
% Supporting Delay fish showing the corresponding learned suppression
An example Delay fish (\FIG{fig1} E; final panel assignment to confirm) showed a corresponding reduction in vigor during the CS--US interval that developed with training and was expressed in early testing. This example provides a qualitative comparison with the established Delay configuration, while the 3sTrace fish illustrates the central short-trace result.
% Contrast Control fish lacking consistent CS-locked suppression
In contrast, the Control fish did not exhibit a consistent CS-locked modulation of vigor across trials (\FIG{fig1} G; final panel assignment to confirm). Although randomized US timing in the Control protocol complicates strict CS-aligned analysis during training, the early-test suppression observed in the paired examples was absent in the control animal. This contrast supports an interpretation based on the CS--US contingency rather than on non-associative changes in motor output.
