# List of Supplementary Figures

## Supplementary Figure 1 — Optovin-evoked unconditioned responses and activation of baseline movement

Characterizes the behavioral effects of optovin-mediated photostimulation in head-fixed larvae. The figure shows representative tail traces and population measurements following violet-light activation of optovin, including the rapid, high-amplitude unconditioned motor response and the subsequent period of reduced movement. It compares activity before and after stimulation and quantifies how optovin pulses increase the amount, frequency, or vigor of tail movement, thereby establishing optovin as an effective unconditioned stimulus and explaining how it helps place otherwise inactive larvae into a measurable behavioral state.

## Supplementary Figure 2 — Detailed conditioning protocol and maintenance of baseline activity

Provides a trial-by-trial diagram of the complete experimental sequence, including priming/habituation, pre-training, training, catch trials, and testing. It identifies the timing and placement of the short priming US pulses, the longer conditioning US pulses, CS presentations, and inter-trial intervals for the paired and unpaired protocols. The figure also quantifies baseline tail activity across the experiment, demonstrating that the initial and interspersed short US pulses maintain sufficient movement during pre-training and testing to permit detection of conditioned motor suppression.

## Supplementary Figure 3 — Violet-light stimulation without optovin does not support conditioning

Shows the specificity control in which larvae undergo delay-style CS–US pairing with the same 100-ms violet-light pulse but without optovin. Representative responses, trial-wise or block-wise normalized vigor, and comparisons of late pre-training with early and late testing demonstrate that violet light alone neither evokes an effective unconditioned response nor produces reliable conditioned suppression. This supports the conclusion that reinforcement depends on optovin-mediated photostimulation rather than the violet flash itself.

## Supplementary Figure 4 — US-aligned responses in paired and unpaired experiments

Presents behavioral data aligned to US onset to separate the optovin-evoked unconditioned response from the CS-evoked conditioned response. It includes US-aligned views of the representative delay and control larvae shown in Figure 1, responses to short and long US pulses, and population data from the randomized unpaired controls. The paired data show the immediate motor burst and post-US immobility, whereas the controls show no systematic anticipatory increase or suppression before their randomly timed US presentations. These analyses confirm that the learned suppression is locked to the predictive CS–US relationship rather than to other events in the protocol.

## Supplementary Figure 5 — Behavioral coverage and quality control for population heatmaps

Documents how much of the population contributes to each time bin in the scaled-vigor heatmaps. Heatmaps or time courses show the number or percentage of fish with measurable tail movement in each bin for the delay, 3-s trace, 10-s trace, and matched-control groups. Corresponding raw-vigor or activity-probability plots clarify that vigor is undefined during immobility and that displayed averages represent the active subset of larvae. This figure supports interpretation of low-activity periods, particularly the post-US immobility phase and the extended 10-s trace interval.

## Supplementary Figure 6 — Individual learning trajectories and heterogeneous expression of conditioned suppression

Shows individual normalized-vigor trajectories across pre-training, training, early testing, and late testing for delay, 3-s trace, 10-s trace, and matched unpaired-control larvae. Paired within-fish changes and representative strong, weak, intermediate, and absent responses illustrate variation in the magnitude and persistence of conditioned suppression. The figure reports continuous learning scores and classifier assignments where applicable, including the number of classifier-positive animals and false positives in controls. The 10-s trace data are presented as weak and heterogeneous, without treating isolated responders as evidence of population-level conditioning.

## Supplementary Figure 7 — Additional conditioned-response timing and catch-trial analyses

Provides supporting analyses of when conditioned suppression occurs. It includes single-fish and pooled catch-trial traces, trial- or block-resolved response development, and CS-aligned and expected-US-aligned representations for delay and 3-s trace conditioning. Representative single-trial traces show that suppression can begin during the CS and extend into the 3-s stimulus-free interval, while test-block data show its subsequent extinction. Alternative timing measures or analysis windows assess the robustness of response onset, peak, duration, and alignment conclusions independently of learner classification.

## Supplementary Figure 8 — Robustness across visual-CS conditions

Separates larvae trained with the red visual CS from the pooled red- and white-CS dataset. Population heatmaps, normalized-vigor trajectories, block comparisons, and matched controls test whether delay and short-trace learning remain detectable when analysis is restricted to the red-CS experiments intended for compatibility with future neural imaging. This analysis establishes that the principal behavioral conclusions are not driven by pooling the two visual-stimulus configurations.
