# Paper Consistency Rules

Status: draft reference only. Do not apply these rules automatically yet.

## Purpose

Maintain a coherent scientific narrative across the manuscript, figures, supplementary figures, Results drafts, Methods, abstract, and planning documents.

## Canonical terminology

- Use **larvae** for the study population and **fish** only when discussing an individual animal or an example fish.
- Use **CS** for conditioned stimulus, **US** for unconditioned stimulus, **CR** for conditioned response, and **UR** for unconditioned response after defining each abbreviation once in the relevant section.
- Use **Delay**, **3sTrace**, and **10sTrace** consistently as condition names.
- Use **Pre-Train (PT)**, **Early Test (ET)**, and **Late Test (LT)** for the predefined five-trial blocks.
- Use **tail-movement vigor** for the behavioral quantity. Define the exact normalized or baseline-relative measure once in Methods and use one name consistently thereafter.
- Use **paired** and **unpaired control** to distinguish contingencies. Avoid treating the unpaired control as merely an inactive condition.

## Claims and evidence

- Every Results claim must be traceable to a figure, table, supplementary figure, or a clearly identified analysis.
- Match claim strength to the available evidence:
  - Use **associated with**, **consistent with**, or **was not detected** when evidence is observational or statistically inconclusive.
  - Use **supports associative learning** only when paired groups are compared with matched unpaired controls.
  - Use **temporal bridging** for suppression that extends beyond CS offset into the trace interval.
  - Use **predictive timing** only after an analysis independently demonstrates alignment with expected US timing or a response shift with the trained CS--US interval.
- Do not describe 10sTrace as successful long-trace conditioning unless a robust, matched population-level analysis supports that conclusion.
- Do not describe the 10sTrace result as an absolute temporal limit; it is a boundary result under the tested protocol.
- Separate a direct observation from an interpretation in adjacent sentences when needed.

## Figure and source consistency

- Every in-text figure citation must use the final figure number and panel range.
- Verify that every panel mentioned in the text exists and displays the stated condition, alignment, metric, and trial phase.
- Keep Figure 1 focused on setup, protocol, and representative individual examples: primary 3sTrace, supporting Delay, and control.
- Keep Figure 2 organized as three population-level columns: Delay, 3sTrace, and 10sTrace, each with its matched control where applicable.
- Use supplementary figures for validation, individual variability, US-aligned controls, classifier robustness, and timing sensitivity analyses.
- Cite external sources for prior knowledge, methods adopted from prior work, and mechanistic interpretation. Do not cite external literature as support for a result generated in this study.

## Section boundaries

- **Introduction:** motivation, prior evidence, knowledge gap, and study question; no detailed Results interpretation.
- **Results:** what was measured, what was observed, and limited evidence-based interpretation.
- **Methods:** operational definitions, exclusions, normalization, timing, response windows, and statistical procedures.
- **Discussion:** implications, limitations, comparison with prior work, and future directions.

## Internal checks before editing

- Do condition names, timings, trial counts, and stimulus durations match Methods and Figure 1?
- Does the text distinguish scaled vigor, normalized vigor, and the fraction of active larvae?
- Does every statistical statement identify the comparison, unit of analysis, and relevant phase?
- Does every figure placeholder have an explicit TODO until finalized?
- Do abstract, Results, Discussion, and figure legends make the same claim with the same level of certainty?
