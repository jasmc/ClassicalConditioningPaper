# Manuscript Roadmap

The behavioral foundation and learner-classification/CR-dynamics plan previously listed as Milestones 1 and 2 have been integrated into `Helpers/Paper.md`. This document retains the prospective mechanistic, memory-persistence, and imaging milestones.

The behavioral manuscript is the clearest minimum paper. The learner-classification and CR-dynamics expansion is appropriate only if its timing and cross-condition analyses are strong. The mechanistic milestone should wait until the motor confounds in the MK-801 experiments and the interpretive controls for Purkinje-cell ablation are resolved. Imaging should be reserved for a genuinely integrated imaging-and-causality story rather than used to enlarge the behavioral manuscript with preliminary data.

## Milestone 3 — Mechanistic sensitivity and memory persistence

### Working title

Molecular and circuit contributions to delay and trace conditioning in larval zebrafish. Two qualifications apply. First, the word contributions is conditional: it may be used only if at least one perturbation is dissociable from general sensorimotor impairment. If the MK-801 motor confounds and the Purkinje-cell validation controls remain unresolved, the title should state sensitivity rather than contribution. Second, this title covers only the perturbation arm. The milestone as currently specified also contains retention, puromycin, and reacquisition experiments, which fall outside it. Either the title should be broadened to cover memory persistence, or those experiments should move to a separate milestone.

### Primary questions

Is acquisition of the conditioned response sensitive to N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) blockade or Purkinje-cell perturbation? Does the learned response remain detectable after a 3-h retention interval, and is that retained response altered by puromycin? Is there evidence of facilitated reacquisition after extinction?

### Minimum evidence required

Each extension must test one defined question using its own matched controls and claim-limiting analyses. Pharmacological experiments require concentration-matched unpaired controls, vehicle matching, acquisition and early-test analyses, baseline-movement measurements, and characterization of the unconditioned response (UR) and other sensorimotor effects. Ablation experiments require the appropriate transgene-negative and treatment-matched controls, verification of the manipulation, and baseline and UR checks. Retention experiments require matched conditioned and unpaired groups, a clearly defined retention interval, documentation of pre-test priming and its consequences, and comparison with immediate testing where appropriate. Puromycin experiments require concentration-matched conditioned and control groups, evidence that acquisition and behavioral performance remain measurable, and cautious interpretation if translation inhibition has not been independently verified. Savings analyses require complete extinction before retraining and direct comparison of initial acquisition, reacquisition, and first-time acquisition in the appropriate control history.

### Central claim

The established conditioning assay can be extended to test molecular, circuit, and memory-related constraints, but the conclusions differ in strength. High-dose MK-801 is associated with loss of conditioned suppression, although substantial motor and UR abnormalities prevent a clean attribution to associative-learning mechanisms. A 3-h retained response is detectable under the modified delay-conditioning protocol, but its resistance to 5 mg/L puromycin does not establish either protein-synthesis-dependent long-term memory or protein-synthesis independence. The present reacquisition experiment provides no significant evidence of savings. Purkinje-cell ablation should support a circuit-level claim only after manipulation verification and sensorimotor controls are complete.

This structure replaces the broad “other extensions” framing with three explicit questions: mechanistic sensitivity, memory persistence, and reacquisition. The current data do not support combining those results into a single complete account of acquisition, consolidation, retention, and circuit mechanism.

### Interpretation and limits

This milestone retains the complete behavioral foundation established in Milestones 1 and 2: assay validation, matched randomized unpaired controls, robust delay and 3sTrace conditioning, learner classification, and the temporal characterization of the conditioned response. Any mechanistic claim rests on that foundation and cannot be stronger than it.

Constrained language is required throughout. Acceptable phrasing takes the form: NMDAR antagonism impaired conditioning, although sensorimotor effects constrain mechanistic interpretation. Do not claim that Purkinje cells are required for the association unless the ablation is selective, validated, controlled, and dissociable from general motor impairment. The behavioral readout is itself a reduction in movement vigor, so any manipulation that reduces movement can mimic a learning deficit; separating an acquisition deficit from a failure of response expression is therefore the interpretive burden of this milestone. Note also that the predictive framing of the conditioned response inherited from earlier milestones remains conditional on the Milestone 2 timing tests and should not be asserted here as established.

### Out of scope

Excluded here: calcium imaging and any neural-dynamics analysis, which belong to Milestone 4; any combined claim that the molecular and circuit mechanism of temporal association has been identified, since MK-801 and Purkinje-cell ablation are two separate experiments with different interpretive limitations; and the 10sTrace condition, which remains a supplementary boundary analysis and should not be reused as a mechanistic extension.

### Main risk

This milestone should not be attempted until the motor confounds in the MK-801 experiments and the interpretive controls for Purkinje-cell ablation are resolved. Attempting it earlier risks a manuscript whose mechanistic claim is immediately discounted by reviewers on confound grounds, while also diluting the behavioral contribution that stands on its own. A second risk is thematic coherence: mechanistic sensitivity, memory persistence, and reacquisition are three distinct questions, and the current data do not support combining them into a single account of acquisition, consolidation, retention, and circuit mechanism. If the perturbation arm is not defensible, the retention and puromycin results are better reported separately than used to pad a mechanistic paper.

### Short pitch

This manuscript combines a validated head-fixed delay and trace-conditioning paradigm with targeted perturbation of candidate learning mechanisms and with tests of memory persistence. The behavioral evidence establishes learned suppression of tail-movement vigor across short stimulus-free intervals, while pharmacological and cellular manipulations test whether defined molecular or cerebellar components contribute to acquisition or expression, and a three-hour retention protocol tests how long the learned response persists. Conclusions are constrained throughout by explicit analyses of baseline behavior, unconditioned responses, and motor competence, and by the absence of independent verification for some manipulations.

### Main figures

#### Figure 5 — Mechanistic sensitivity of delay and 3sTrace conditioning

This should be the principal mechanistic figure and should contain two clearly separated sections.

##### NMDAR-antagonist experiment

Report the MK-801 experimental design and treatment schedule; delay conditioning at 0, 10, and 100 µM MK-801; 3sTrace conditioning at 0 and 100 µM MK-801; concentration-matched unpaired controls; acquisition across training; late-pre-training versus early-test quantification; extinction during testing; sample sizes and exclusions; and the principal baseline and UR analyses required to interpret loss of the conditioned response.

Delay conditioning remained measurable at 10 µM but was impaired at 100 µM. In 3sTrace, conditioned suppression was absent at 100 µM. However, the high concentration also altered baseline behavior, tail-movement structure, UR intensity and latency, and forward swimming. Only the delay experiment included pre-incubation, so the delay and 3sTrace drug effects are not a fully matched comparison.

The main claim should therefore be that conditioned suppression is sensitive to high-dose MK-801, but the accompanying sensorimotor abnormalities prevent the behavioral deficit from being attributed exclusively to impaired associative learning.

Avoid the statement that NMDAR signaling is required for delay and trace learning. That wording is presently too strong because failure to express the behavioral response could arise partly from drug-induced performance impairment. The available data support pharmacological sensitivity, not yet a selective molecular requirement for acquisition.

##### Purkinje-cell ablation experiment

Report the ablation design; delay and 3sTrace conditioning; matched unpaired groups; ablation-positive and genetic or treatment-control groups; acquisition and early-test behavioral effects; baseline motor activity; UR probability, intensity, and latency; and sample sizes and exclusions.

The current materials describe a nitroreductase–Nifurpirinol manipulation and report impaired learning in the ablation group, but the retrieved passages do not provide the complete verification, sample-size, or sensorimotor analyses needed to evaluate the specificity of that result. The circuit conclusion should therefore remain conditional.

The defensible claim is that Purkinje-cell ablation is associated with impaired expression of conditioned suppression. A specific requirement for Purkinje cells in associative acquisition depends on verification that the manipulation was effective and did not disrupt detection of the CS, response to the US, baseline movement, or capacity to express the CR.

Do not combine MK-801 and ablation into a general claim that the molecular and circuit mechanism has been established. They are two perturbation experiments with different interpretive limitations.

#### Figure 6 — Three-hour retention and puromycin sensitivity

Use this as a focused memory-persistence figure rather than a general “long-term memory” figure.

Report the modified spaced-training protocol; the 3-h interval between training and testing; the pre-test priming procedure; conditioned and matched unpaired groups at 0 and 5 mg/L puromycin; acquisition before the retention interval; conditioned responding during early post-retention testing; extinction during later testing; individual and population-level retention results; baseline movement before and after pre-test priming; the response to the priming USs; sample sizes and exclusions; and a direct statement that translational inhibition was not independently verified.

The protocol used a 10-min break after every 10 training trials, a minimum 3-h retention interval, and four 100-ms US presentations before testing to restore sufficient movement for measuring suppression. The analyzed groups were 19 control and 27 delay fish at 0 mg/L puromycin, and 12 control and 9 delay fish at 5 mg/L; exclusions were also reported.

The central result should be stated as follows: a conditioned response remains detectable after a 3-h retention interval under the modified delay-conditioning protocol, and treatment with 5 mg/L puromycin did not produce conclusive evidence of reduced retention.

Do not state that the 3-h memory is protein-synthesis independent. The experiment did not verify effective translational inhibition in the relevant tissue, and the puromycin-treated conditioned group did not itself show a statistically conclusive retained response in the reported phase comparison. The result is therefore inconclusive regarding protein-synthesis dependence.

Also avoid calling the result canonical long-term memory. thesis 1.pdf deliberately uses the more neutral term long-lasting memory, because the response was not blocked by the tested puromycin condition and spaced training did not show a demonstrated advantage.

## Milestone 4 — Imaging

### Working title

Brain-wide dynamics and candidate mechanisms of temporal associative learning in larval zebrafish. The phrase candidate mechanisms is conditional on at least one interpretable causal perturbation being carried forward from Milestone 3. If no perturbation survives its sensorimotor controls, the title should refer to brain-wide dynamics alone, and the milestone becomes a descriptive imaging contribution rather than an integrated mechanistic one.

### Primary question

What neural activity accompanies acquisition, learner status, and suppression timed to the expected US?

### Minimum evidence required

This milestone retains the validated behavioral assay, robust delay and 3sTrace conditioning, the temporal characterization of the conditioned response, and, where available, at least one interpretable causal perturbation from Milestone 3. It additionally requires publication-ready imaging in conditioned and control fish; behavior quantified in the imaging preparation; sufficient biological replication in both conditioned and control animals; motion correction, segmentation, anatomical registration, trial alignment, exclusion criteria, and signal-quality checks; separation of sensory, US-related, motor, baseline-state, learning-related, and timing-related activity; replicated neural signals that change with acquisition or that predict conditioned-response expression; and neural–behavioral analyses linked explicitly to the earlier milestones.

### Central claim

Replicated neural population dynamics are associated with acquisition, conditioned responding, learner status, and—if Milestone 2.2 is passed—expected US timing. The imaging identifies candidate learning- and timing-related populations but does not establish circuit necessity by itself.

### Interpretation and limits

Imaging correlations alone do not identify a required circuit. Use learning-related activity, candidate populations, or associated with conditioned responding unless convergent causal evidence supports stronger wording. Activity that changes across training may reflect the learned association, but it may equally reflect altered movement, arousal, or baseline state, so the analyses separating these sources are what license the interpretation rather than decorating it. Any claim about activity timed to the expected US inherits the conditional status of the Milestone 2 timing tests and cannot be stated more strongly than the behavioral result it rests on.

### Out of scope

Excluded here: any statement that a specific circuit is necessary for temporal association on the basis of imaging alone; new mechanistic experiments that have not passed the Milestone 3 controls; and preliminary or pilot imaging used to enlarge the behavioral manuscript. Imaging enters the roadmap only as a complete contribution in its own right.

### Main risk

This milestone should be reserved for a genuinely integrated imaging-and-causality story rather than used to enlarge the behavioral manuscript with preliminary imaging. The characteristic failure mode is an underpowered imaging dataset appended to a strong behavioral result, which weakens both. A second risk is that the imaging preparation constrains behavior: if head-fixed larvae move less under the microscope, the suppression readout may lose the dynamic range that the behavioral milestones depend on, and this must be quantified in the imaging preparation rather than assumed from the behavioral rig.

### Short pitch

This manuscript combines a head-fixed delay and trace-conditioning assay with brain-wide neural recording and causal perturbation to investigate how temporally separated events become associated in the larval zebrafish brain. Learned suppression of tail-movement vigor provides the behavioral readout, learner classification resolves which individuals express the association, and population imaging identifies learning-related neural dynamics that change with acquisition or predict conditioned responding. Targeted manipulation then tests the contribution of candidate molecular or circuit mechanisms, with claims constrained to candidate populations wherever causal evidence is absent.

# Supplementary figures

## Milestone 3 — Mechanistic sensitivity and memory persistence

### Figure 5 — Mechanistic sensitivity of delay and 3sTrace conditioning

#### Supplementary Figure S11 — MK-801 baseline and sensorimotor effects

Include the complete analyses needed to determine whether the drug changed the ability to perform the behavioral assay: fraction of fish moving; number of movements; fraction of time moving; baseline vigor; valid-trial frequency; UR latency; UR vigor and duration; post-US immobility; tail-movement structure; representative cumulative tail-angle traces; and health or final-US response checks at each concentration.

This is essential rather than decorative. At 100 µM, MK-801 altered movement organization, attenuated and delayed the UR, and caused near-loss of forward swims. Because the CR is itself defined as reduced vigor, these effects directly constrain interpretation of the learning result.

#### Supplementary Figure S12 — MK-801 dosing, treatment comparability, and robustness

Include all concentration-matched conditioned and control groups; individual-fish trajectories; complete trial-block traces; the 10 µM delay result; the 100 µM delay and 3sTrace results; treatment and pre-incubation timelines; vehicle concentration; exclusions by condition; sensitivity to normalization and inclusion criteria; and analyses separating an acquisition deficit from failure of CR expression.

The figure should explicitly document that delay larvae, but not the reported 3sTrace larvae, underwent MK-801 pre-incubation. A direct claim that trace learning is more NMDAR-dependent than delay learning requires a matched dose-response experiment, including an intermediate concentration in 3sTrace.

#### Supplementary Figure S13 — Purkinje-cell ablation validation and behavioral controls

Include evidence of Purkinje-cell ablation; anatomical extent and variability of the manipulation; ablation-positive and transgene-negative treatment controls; matched unpaired groups; baseline movement; CS responsiveness before training; UR reliability, vigor, and latency; health checks; exclusions; individual learning trajectories; and any relationship between ablation extent and behavioral effect.

Until these controls are complete, the result should be described as an ablation-associated behavioral impairment, not definitive proof that Purkinje cells are selectively required for forming the association. The current manuscript describes six comparison groups, but the retrieved text does not completely document the validation and performance-control evidence.

### Figure 6 — Three-hour retention and puromycin sensitivity

#### Supplementary Figure S14 — Three-hour retention: baseline restoration and performance controls

Include baseline activity before the retention interval; activity at the end of the 3-h interval; the effect of each pre-test priming US; activity at the start of retention testing; UR responses before and after the retention interval; valid-trial counts; conditioned and unpaired groups; 0 and 5 mg/L puromycin groups; and comparison with the corresponding immediate-test assay, while clearly acknowledging protocol differences.

This analysis is necessary because low activity after the retention interval motivated the four 100-ms US presentations before testing. Those USs enabled measurement of suppression but also changed the immediate pre-test experience. Consequently, the result demonstrates retention under the re-primed test protocol, not an uncontaminated measure of spontaneous memory expression after 3 h.

#### Supplementary Figure S15 — Puromycin controls and claim boundaries

Include acquisition under 0 and 5 mg/L puromycin; matched unpaired groups; baseline and UR measures; exposure and washout timeline; sample sizes and exclusions; individual retention trajectories; effect-size estimates with uncertainty; any completed immediate-test puromycin control; and, if available, independent evidence that the treatment inhibited protein synthesis.

Preliminary 10 mg/L observations should not enter the principal result unless they have adequate matched unpaired controls and sufficient sample size. The current documents explicitly characterize that experiment as underpowered and lacking full controls.

### Not tied to a single main figure

#### Supplementary Figure S16 — Reacquisition and memory savings

Include the extinction trajectory preceding retraining; initial acquisition in the conditioned group; reacquisition in the same fish; first-time paired training in animals with the relevant control history; prespecified learning-rate or trials-to-criterion analyses; group and individual trajectories; classifier-independent all-fish analyses; baseline and UR checks during retraining; and explicit consideration of prior unpaired CS–US exposure in the comparison group.

The current analysis found no significant difference between initial acquisition and reacquisition, and therefore no behavioral evidence of savings under the tested protocol. This is a valid negative result, but it does not establish that no latent memory remained: complete extinction, limited measurement sensitivity, extensive US exposure, or the history of the comparison animals could have affected reacquisition.

#### Supplementary Figure S17 — Secondary boundary and robustness extensions

Place the following here rather than in the principal Milestone 3 figures: 10sTrace as a boundary condition; massed-versus-spaced pilot comparisons; alternative training strength or reinforcement schedules; preliminary higher-dose puromycin experiments; longer retention attempts; and additional extinction analyses.

The 10sTrace result already belongs conceptually with the temporal-boundary analysis of Milestone 1/2 and should not be reused as a central mechanistic extension. The present data describe weak or non-significant population-level evidence at the 10-s interval.
