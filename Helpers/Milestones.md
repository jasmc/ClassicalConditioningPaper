# Manuscript Roadmap

The manuscript develops through five evidence milestones. Milestone 1 establishes the behavioral foundation: delay conditioning and 3sTrace conditioning are present. Milestone 2.1 identifies learners rigorously. Milestone 2.2 tests whether learner suppression is timed to the expected US—whether the fish know when the US should occur. Milestone 3 adds retention and mechanistic extensions. Milestone 4 adds imaging.

Default: Plan 1. Preferred expansion: Plan 2. Plan 1 is the clearest minimum paper because the assay refinement, delay conditioning, and short-trace conditioning form one coherent contribution. Plan 2 is the most attractive extension if timing and cross-condition analyses are already strong. Plan 3 should wait until the motor confounds in the MK-801 experiments or the interpretive controls for Purkinje-cell ablation are resolved. Plan 4 should be reserved for a genuinely integrated imaging-and-causality story rather than using preliminary imaging to enlarge the behavioral manuscript.

## Milestone 1 — Delay and short-trace conditioning

### Working title

A head-fixed classical conditioning paradigm for delay and trace conditioning in larval zebrafish.

### Primary question

Can head-fixed larval zebrafish acquire delay and 3sTrace associations, expressed as a learned change in tail-movement vigor?

### Minimum evidence required

The main figures should establish the behavioral preparation and demonstrate learning at both the individual and population levels. They should include the head-fixed preparation with free tail movement; the visual conditioned stimulus (CS) and optovin-mediated unconditioned stimulus (US); validation of robust optovin-evoked tail responses; the priming/habituation procedure used to maintain a sufficiently active baseline; the delay, 3sTrace, 10sTrace, and matched randomized unpaired-control protocols; representative raw data from individual larvae; population heatmaps; trial-by-trial changes in vigor following CS onset to show acquisition and extinction; and comparisons of late pre-training with early testing to quantify learning. Sample sizes, exclusions, normalization procedures, response windows, and statistical tests must be reported either in the figure legends or Methods.

### Central claim

Head-fixed larval zebrafish acquire delay and short-trace associations, expressed as learned suppression of tail-movement vigor. Delay conditioning validates the assay, whereas 3sTrace conditioning provides the central behavioral advance by demonstrating association across a stimulus-free interval. The 10sTrace condition tests the boundary of this capacity and should be presented more cautiously because the available analyses indicate weaker and less consistent learning than for delay and 3sTrace conditioning.

### Interpretation and limits

The trace result supports association across a short stimulus-free interval. It does not, by itself, establish working memory or identify the neural mechanism that bridges the interval. For this reason the milestone claims learned suppression rather than predictive suppression: the stronger, predictive wording requires the timing analyses reserved for Milestone 2 and should not be adopted here. One scope decision remains open and should be fixed before drafting: whether 10sTrace is retained as a full main condition, as currently specified in Figures 1 and 2, or demoted to a concise supplementary boundary condition alongside Supplementary Figure S17. The strong trace condition is reported as 3sTrace throughout.

### Out of scope

The following are excluded from this milestone and belong to later ones: MK-801 and other pharmacological manipulations; Purkinje-cell ablation; three-hour retention; puromycin; savings and reacquisition; learner classification; and imaging data. The 10sTrace condition is excluded from the central claim and, if retained at all, should appear only as a concise boundary condition.

### Main risk

Novelty depends on the trace-conditioning evidence being both statistically and behaviorally convincing. Delay conditioning has been demonstrated previously in larval zebrafish and validates the assay rather than establishing the advance.

### Short pitch

This manuscript introduces a head-fixed larval zebrafish assay for classical conditioning using a visual conditioned stimulus and an optovin-mediated unconditioned stimulus. Brief unpaired optovin stimuli establish a sufficiently active behavioral baseline to reveal a suppressive conditioned response. Larvae reduce tail-movement vigor after conditioned-stimulus onset in both delay and short-trace protocols, and the suppression develops across training and extinguishes when the unconditioned stimulus is withheld, at both the individual and population levels. Together these results provide a behavioral foundation for future mechanistic and circuit-level studies of temporal associative learning.

## Milestone 2 — Learner classification and CR dynamics

### Working title

Predictive motor suppression during delay and trace conditioning in head-fixed larval zebrafish. The word predictive is conditional: it may be used only if the timing analyses specified below show that suppression varies with the trained CS–US interval or aligns more consistently with the expected US than with CS onset. Otherwise the title should revert to learned, timed, or temporally organized suppression.

### Primary questions

Which individual fish show evidence of learning? Among classified learners, how does the conditioned response (CR) develop and when is learned behavioral suppression expressed relative to the expected unconditioned stimulus (US)?

### Minimum evidence required

This milestone retains the assay validation, matched randomized controls, delay-conditioning evidence, and 3sTrace evidence established in Milestone 1, and adds the following. A transparent and reproducible learner-classification approach; a prespecified continuous learning measure; classifier inputs, score distributions, threshold or uncertainty estimates, and validation against matched unpaired controls; learner fractions by condition; representative learner and non-learner fish; and corresponding analyses using all eligible fish. CR dynamics should then be investigated in catch trials and/or early test trials without US contamination, with learner-resolved and all-fish response time courses; analyses aligned separately to conditioned-stimulus (CS) onset, CS offset, and expected US onset; direct comparison of delay and 3sTrace conditioning for both acquisition and response timing; learning dynamics resolved at the trial or training-block level; temporal evolution of the response across training; and timing measures that are independent of the features used for learner classification. The 10sTrace condition enters this milestone only as a secondary or supplementary boundary condition, and only if the data are interpretable.

### Central claim

Learning is heterogeneous across fish, and a reproducible classification identifies individuals that express the conditioned behavioral change. Learner-resolved analysis then characterizes the dynamics of learned suppression, including its development during training and its expression relative to the expected US. Evidence that suppression extends into the trace interval supports temporal bridging. Stronger evidence for predictive timing requires suppression to vary with the trained CS–US interval or to align more consistently with the expected US than with CS onset.

### Interpretation and limits

The last qualification is important because the current analyses show that suppression changes over training and, in 3sTrace fish, extends into the stimulus-free interval. However, the existing materials also note that CR onset can shift toward CS onset; therefore, expected-US timing should be tested directly rather than inferred from visual alignment alone. Temporal bridging and predictive timing are separate conclusions, and only the first is presently supported.

A weak or absent 10sTrace result should be described as a practical boundary under the tested conditions, not as proof that larvae cannot learn across a 10-s interval. Insufficient training, limited measurement sensitivity, and baseline-activity constraints are all plausible alternative explanations. Learner classification should likewise be presented as a description of behavioral heterogeneity rather than evidence that learners and non-learners form discrete biological populations.

### Out of scope

The following belong to later milestones and are excluded here: MK-801 and other pharmacological manipulations; Purkinje-cell ablation; three-hour retention; puromycin; savings and reacquisition; and imaging data. Any neural or circuit interpretation of learner status or response timing is deferred to Milestone 4.

### Main risk

The principal risk is circularity between learner classification and timing analysis: if the classifier selects fish using a window that ends at the expected US, that same window cannot then establish that suppression is timed to the expected US. A secondary risk is statistical power, because catch trials are few and learner-resolved subgroups are small, which may leave the delay-versus-3sTrace timing comparison inconclusive. If the timing tests fail, the milestone still stands as a learner-classification and CR-dynamics contribution, but the predictive framing must be withdrawn from the title and central claim.

### Short pitch

This manuscript establishes that head-fixed larval zebrafish acquire delay and 3sTrace associations and characterizes the resulting conditioned response as a temporally organized suppression of tail-movement vigor. A transparent, prespecified classification resolves the heterogeneity of learning across individuals, and learner-resolved catch-trial analyses describe how suppression develops across training and when it is expressed relative to CS onset, CS offset, and the expected unconditioned stimulus. Direct comparison of the delay and 3sTrace protocols defines how learning and response timing vary with conditioned-stimulus–unconditioned-stimulus separation, while the 10-s condition provides a cautious test of the assay’s temporal boundary.

## Milestone 3 — Mechanistic sensitivity and memory persistence

### Working title

Molecular and circuit contributions to delay and trace conditioning in larval zebrafish. Two qualifications apply. First, the word contributions is conditional: it may be used only if at least one perturbation is dissociable from general sensorimotor impairment. If the MK-801 motor confounds and the Purkinje-cell validation controls remain unresolved, the title should state sensitivity rather than contribution. Second, this title covers only the perturbation arm. The milestone as currently specified also contains retention, puromycin, and reacquisition experiments, which fall outside it. Either the title should be broadened to cover memory persistence, or those experiments should move to a separate milestone.

### Primary questions

Is acquisition of the conditioned response sensitive to N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor (NMDAR) blockade or Purkinje-cell perturbation? Does the learned response remain detectable after a 3-h retention interval, and is that retained response altered by puromycin? Is there evidence of facilitated reacquisition after extinction?

### Minimum evidence required

Each extension must test one defined question using its own matched controls and claim-limiting analyses. Pharmacological experiments require concentration-matched unpaired controls, vehicle matching, acquisition and early-test analyses, baseline-movement measurements, and characterization of the unconditioned response (UR) and other sensorimotor effects. Ablation experiments require the appropriate transgene-negative and treatment-matched controls, verification of the manipulation, and baseline and UR checks. Retention experiments require matched conditioned and unpaired groups, a clearly defined retention interval, documentation of pre-test priming and its consequences, and comparison with immediate testing where appropriate. Puromycin experiments require concentration-matched conditioned and control groups, evidence that acquisition and behavioral performance remain measurable, and cautious interpretation if translation inhibition has not been independently verified. Savings analyses require complete extinction before retraining and direct comparison of initial acquisition, reacquisition, and first-time acquisition in the appropriate control history.

### Central claim

The established conditioning assay can be extended to test molecular, circuit, and memory-related constraints, but the conclusions differ in strength. High-dose MK-801 is associated with loss of conditioned suppression, although substantial motor and UR abnormalities prevent a clean attribution to associative-learning mechanisms. A 3-h retained response is detectable under the modified delay-conditioning protocol, but its resistance to 5 mg/L puromycin does not establish either protein-synthesis-dependent long-term memory or protein-synthesis independence. The present reacquisition experiment provides no significant evidence of savings. Purkinje-cell ablation should support a circuit-level claim only after manipulation verification and sensorimotor controls are complete.

This structure replaces the broad “other extensions” framing with three explicit questions: mechanistic sensitivity, memory persistence, and reacquisition. The current data do not support combining those results into a single complete account of acquisition, consolidation, retention, and circuit mechanism.

### Interpretation and limits

This milestone retains the complete behavioral foundation established in Milestones 1 and 2: assay validation, matched randomized unpaired controls, robust delay and 3sTrace conditioning, learner classification, and the temporal characterization of the conditioned response. Any mechanistic claim rests on that foundation and cannot be stronger than it.

Constrained language is required throughout. Acceptable phrasing takes the form: NMDAR antagonism impaired conditioning, although sensorimotor effects constrain mechanistic interpretation. Do not claim that Purkinje cells are required for the association unless the ablation is selective, validated, controlled, and dissociable from general motor impairment. The behavioral readout is itself a reduction in movement vigor, so any manipulation that reduces movement can mimic a learning deficit; separating an acquisition deficit from a failure of response expression is therefore the interpretive burden of this milestone. Note also that the predictive framing of the conditioned response inherited from earlier milestones remains conditional on the Milestone 2 timing tests and should not be asserted here as established.

### Out of scope

Excluded here: calcium imaging and any neural-dynamics analysis, which belong to Milestone 4; any combined claim that the molecular and circuit mechanism of temporal association has been identified, since MK-801 and Purkinje-cell ablation are two separate experiments with different interpretive limitations; and the 10sTrace condition, which remains a supplementary boundary analysis and should not be reused as a mechanistic extension.

### Main risk

This milestone should not be attempted until the motor confounds in the MK-801 experiments and the interpretive controls for Purkinje-cell ablation are resolved. Attempting it earlier risks a manuscript whose mechanistic claim is immediately discounted by reviewers on confound grounds, while also diluting the behavioral contribution that stands on its own. A second risk is thematic coherence: mechanistic sensitivity, memory persistence, and reacquisition are three distinct questions, and the current data do not support combining them into a single account of acquisition, consolidation, retention, and circuit mechanism. If the perturbation arm is not defensible, the retention and puromycin results are better reported separately than used to pad a mechanistic paper.

### Short pitch

This manuscript combines a validated head-fixed delay and trace-conditioning paradigm with targeted perturbation of candidate learning mechanisms and with tests of memory persistence. The behavioral evidence establishes learned suppression of tail-movement vigor across short stimulus-free intervals, while pharmacological and cellular manipulations test whether defined molecular or cerebellar components contribute to acquisition or expression, and a three-hour retention protocol tests how long the learned response persists. Conclusions are constrained throughout by explicit analyses of baseline behavior, unconditioned responses, and motor competence, and by the absence of independent verification for some manipulations.

## Milestone 4 — Imaging

### Working title

Brain-wide dynamics and candidate mechanisms of temporal associative learning in larval zebrafish. The phrase candidate mechanisms is conditional on at least one interpretable causal perturbation being carried forward from Milestone 3. If no perturbation survives its sensorimotor controls, the title should refer to brain-wide dynamics alone, and the milestone becomes a descriptive imaging contribution rather than an integrated mechanistic one.

### Primary question

What neural activity accompanies acquisition, learner status, and suppression timed to the expected US?

### Minimum evidence required

This milestone retains the validated behavioral assay, robust delay and 3sTrace conditioning, the temporal characterization of the conditioned response, and, where available, at least one interpretable causal perturbation from Milestone 3. It additionally requires publication-ready imaging in conditioned and control fish; behavior quantified in the imaging preparation; sufficient biological replication in both conditioned and control animals; motion correction, segmentation, anatomical registration, trial alignment, exclusion criteria, and signal-quality checks; separation of sensory, US-related, motor, baseline-state, learning-related, and timing-related activity; replicated neural signals that change with acquisition or that predict conditioned-response expression; and neural–behavioral analyses linked explicitly to the earlier milestones.

### Central claim

Replicated neural population dynamics are associated with acquisition, conditioned responding, learner status, and—if Milestone 2.2 is passed—expected US timing. The imaging identifies candidate learning- and timing-related populations but does not establish circuit necessity by itself.

### Interpretation and limits

Imaging correlations alone do not identify a required circuit. Use learning-related activity, candidate populations, or associated with conditioned responding unless convergent causal evidence supports stronger wording. Activity that changes across training may reflect the learned association, but it may equally reflect altered movement, arousal, or baseline state, so the analyses separating these sources are what license the interpretation rather than decorating it. Any claim about activity timed to the expected US inherits the conditional status of the Milestone 2 timing tests and cannot be stated more strongly than the behavioral result it rests on.

### Out of scope

Excluded here: any statement that a specific circuit is necessary for temporal association on the basis of imaging alone; new mechanistic experiments that have not passed the Milestone 3 controls; and preliminary or pilot imaging used to enlarge the behavioral manuscript. Imaging enters the roadmap only as a complete contribution in its own right.

### Main risk

This milestone should be reserved for a genuinely integrated imaging-and-causality story rather than used to enlarge the behavioral manuscript with preliminary imaging. The characteristic failure mode is an underpowered imaging dataset appended to a strong behavioral result, which weakens both. A second risk is that the imaging preparation constrains behavior: if head-fixed larvae move less under the microscope, the suppression readout may lose the dynamic range that the behavioral milestones depend on, and this must be quantified in the imaging preparation rather than assumed from the behavioral rig.

### Short pitch

This manuscript combines a head-fixed delay and trace-conditioning assay with brain-wide neural recording and causal perturbation to investigate how temporally separated events become associated in the larval zebrafish brain. Learned suppression of tail-movement vigor provides the behavioral readout, learner classification resolves which individuals express the association, and population imaging identifies learning-related neural dynamics that change with acquisition or predict conditioned responding. Targeted manipulation then tests the contribution of candidate molecular or circuit mechanisms, with claims constrained to candidate populations wherever causal evidence is absent.
